About IFC

Built around real family life

Interwoven Family Care is built for the parts of family life that do not fit neatly into a preset service. Support is shaped with families around what works, what feels hard, what people communicate, what the environment demands, and what the caregiver can realistically carry.

The goal is not a polished family or a perfect routine. The goal is more clarity, more support, and more moments when daily life feels workable.

Deeper Philosophy

Care Starts With Understanding

Childcare is one thread in the larger weave of family support. IFC looks at the people, relationships, routines, environment, stress, strengths, values, and capacity around what feels hard.

Every family member matters as an individual and is shaped by relationships, routines, home life, community, capacity, and need. We look at the whole picture so support can fit the actual family in front of us.

Life happens. Schedules shift, routines shift, needs shift, and family capacity changes. Our care is designed to move with your family, without shame, pressure, or extra hoops.

Trust is built over time, especially when support happens inside your home and family life. We show up with consistency, curiosity, respect, and honest communication so families can feel safe being real about what is hard.

Families are not meant to do this alone. Meaningful care includes connection, belonging, community resources, and relationships that help families feel supported beyond one conversation or one visit.

How Support Begins

  1. InquiryTell Bri what feels hard. You do not need the right service name.
  2. Introductory ConversationTalk through the people, routines, goals, schedule, safety, location, and what you have already tried.
  3. Support RecommendationBri recommends a focused session, ongoing support, care-first support, a custom blend, a referral, or another next step.
  4. Written Scope and PriceBefore support begins, receive the service scope, communication and documentation limits, schedule, price, and end or review point.
  5. Agreement and PermissionsComplete the agreements, authorizations, emergency information, and other forms the service requires.
  6. Support BeginsWork together in the agreed lane.
  7. Review and AdjustNotice what is helping, what changed, and whether support should continue, shift, pause, or end.

About Bri

Miss Bri

Little Bri, before she had language for being AuDHD. I know what it can mean to be a child who notices everything, feels deeply, and is still learning how to communicate. That kind of understanding is woven into how I care for children and families.

I’m Brianna “Bri” Waugh, Owner and Family Support Provider. I have worked in early childhood education and family-related care since 2015. My background includes an Associate’s Degree with an early-childhood focus, Reggio Emilia-inspired and Montessori experience, Head Start and Early Head Start, in-home care, and work with neurodivergent children and families with layered needs.

I pay attention to what opens a door: the favorite cup, the song that soothes, the color someone chooses every time, the routine that almost works, and the moment everyone keeps getting stuck. Support has to be specific to the family in front of me.
