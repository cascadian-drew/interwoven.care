Our Approach

Care Starts With Understanding

Childcare is one thread in the larger tapestry of family support. Families are whole systems. A hard morning can shape the whole day. Your exhaustion, your child’s needs, your home setup, your schedule, and your relationships all shape what is possible.

Every family member matters as an individual and is shaped by relationships, routines, home life, community, capacity, and need. We look at the whole picture so support can fit the actual family in front of us.

Life happens. Schedules shift, routines shift, needs shift, and family capacity changes. Our care is designed to move with your family, without shame, pressure, or extra hoops.

Trust is built over time, especially when support happens inside your home and family life. We show up with consistency, curiosity, respect, and honest communication so families can feel safe being real about what is hard.

Families are not meant to do this alone. Meaningful care includes connection, belonging, community resources, and relationships that help families feel supported beyond one conversation or one visit.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you support more complex family needs?

Yes. This is central to our work. We work with families navigating all kinds of complex needs, including Autism, ADHD, sensory needs, developmental differences, and more. Care is shaped through listening, observation, and respect for what each person and the household as a whole are already communicating, so support can grow with real life.

What does support look like?

Support is shaped around your family. It could be during the after-school stretch, when everyone is tired and still needs care, dinner, help winding down, and connection.

In those moments, we can bring another steady adult into your home: playing, helping with transitions, supporting feelings, noticing needs, folding laundry, loading dishes, organizing a space that is not working, or talking through what keeps feeling hardest.

We pay attention to your patterns, stress points, communication style, and what might help tomorrow feel a little smoother.

What does "adaptive" care mean?

Adaptive care changes with your family’s needs, schedule, and capacity. We do not ask families to fit into a box. We build care around your real situation and adjust as your needs change.

What ages do you work with?

Interwoven Family Care supports families with children and youth from birth through 18. Care may include adult caregivers, siblings, and extended family. If you are wondering whether your situation fits, tell us about your household and what you are carrying.

What areas do you serve?

In-home support is available in Tacoma and nearby Pierce County. Virtual Family Coaching is available separately. Not sure whether you are in range? Reach out and we can talk it through.

Is IFC therapy or clinical treatment?

No. We are not therapy, clinical treatment, legal representation, special education representation, formal case management, medical care, or crisis support. We offer practical, relationship-based family support and coaching that can help carry recommendations, routines, and everyday care into real life.

About Bri

Miss Bri

Little Bri, before she had language for being AuDHD. I know what it can mean to be a child who notices everything, feels deeply, and is still learning how to communicate. That kind of understanding is woven into how I care for children and families.

I'm Brianna (Bri) Waugh, Owner & Family Support Provider at Interwoven Family Care™. I built this work after more than a decade alongside children and families in early childhood centers, in-home care, and community programs.

Again and again, my work has brought me alongside children with higher support needs and families holding several kinds of complexity at once. I am comfortable in the thick of family life and am happy to jump in no matter the mess.

My background includes Reggio Emilia-inspired programs, Montessori, Head Start and Early Head Start, in-home care, and community-based family support. I lean towards play-based learning and relationship-centered practices and have deep respect for environments that encourage independence, curiosity, critical thinking, regulation, and belonging.

More than anything, I believe this work has to be specific to your family. I want you to reach out and talk with me, because your family's nuance matters. Your children, your household, your schedule, your worries, your values, and your hopes all shape what care should look like for you.
